Fill in the blanks, one letter at a time.

CipherDesk is the newspaper cryptogram in a desk edition. Every puzzle is a quote hidden behind a substitution cipher: each letter stands for another letter — never itself — and working out which is which reveals the quote, and the person it belongs to. Every picture on this page is a screenshot from the app itself — nothing redrawn.

The app, at a glance

One desk holds everything: the puzzle in the middle, the substitution map at the side keeping every guess, the keyboard below, and the tools across the top — Undo, Hint, Check, Reset, Paste, Link library file, New. The puzzle below is part-solved: two letters are in, and the map at the right already shows them.

One thing to know before you start: the header counts are live — 71 quotes, 9 categories — and every tap on a category tab, even the one already selected, deals a fresh puzzle with a fresh cipher.

How to read the board

Help

This is a real puzzle, solved in the app: “Outside of a dog, a book is man’s best friend. Inside of a dog it’s too dark to read.” — Groucho Marx. A CipherDesk cell is three small things stacked on one letter — numbered here, explained at the side. Once you can read one cell, you can read the whole board.

1

Your guessThe large letter on top — the real letter you think the cipher letter stands for. It appears everywhere that cipher letter appears in the puzzle, so one good guess fills in across the whole quote.

2

The cipher letterThe small letter under your guess — the letter as printed in the encoded quote. The same cipher letter always stands for the same real letter, throughout the puzzle.

3

FrequencyThe tiny number at the bottom — how many times that cipher letter appears in this puzzle. The busiest letters are the place to start: in English they are usually E, T, A hiding underneath.

4

The substitution mapAll the cipher → plain pairings in play, always in view at the side. Every guess you make — and every hint you take — lands here. One plain letter can’t stand for two cipher letters: assigning it moves it.

5

Hints givenUnder the map, a running list of every letter Hint revealed, so a revealed letter is never lost — and you can see exactly how much help you took.

6

The author lineUnder the puzzle sits the quote’s attribution, hidden while you solve. When every letter matches, the “Solved! Well done.” banner appears, the author is revealed in full, and the timer stops.

The one rule

Each letter stands for another letter — never itself — and it stands for that same letter every time it appears. No letter ever encodes as itself, so one whole guess is ruled out before you start: if a cipher letter is T, its answer is never T.

Only the letters A–Z are encoded. Word breaks and punctuation stay exactly where they are, so one-letter words and apostrophes are clues, not decoration.

How to use it — four steps

Help

Solving is the same loop the newspaper version always was — CipherDesk just keeps the bookkeeping. The timer starts with your first guess and stops when you solve.

1

Pick a tab, tap New

Choose All, Saved, or a subject — Wisdom, Science, Leadership, Wit, History, Sports or Comedy. A quote is dealt and encoded with a fresh cipher.

2

Fill in the blanks

Tap a cipher letter, then tap a letter on the keyboard — or type it. Your guess fills in everywhere that letter appears, and the map updates with it.

3

Hint, Check, Undo, Reset

Hint reveals one letter — the most frequent one you haven’t got right yet. Check marks only your wrong letters, in red. Undo takes back the last guess; Reset clears them all.

4

Finish, or print it

When every letter matches, the author is revealed and the timer stops. Prefer paper? The printer button prints just the puzzle — blanks, cipher letters, and the one rule.

Bring your own cryptogramFound one in a newspaper or a book? Tap Paste, paste the ciphertext in, and solve it on the desk. Or type a solution of your own and tap Scramble solution — CipherDesk encodes it into a puzzle for someone else. Keep the solution, and Check can mark wrong letters on that puzzle too.

The Saved library is a file you holdPuzzles you paste or scramble live in the Saved tab. Link library file connects them to saved-puzzles.json on your device — the app loads it, keeps it updated in place, and reconnects on your next visit. On a browser that can’t link a file, Download copy saves the same file instead.

Nine tabs across the top

Tap a tab, then tap New, and a quote from that tab is dealt. All covers the whole library of 71 reviewed quotes; Saved is your own library, separate from it. The counts are the quotes in each subject tab in the current library.
