Prime Factor Finder

Every whole number bigger than 1 is built from prime numbers multiplied together. Here you do the building yourself — divide a number down to its primes, split it into a factor tree, group the primes to find every kind of factor, and line up a fraction’s factors to cancel them away.

Tap any number that isn’t prime and split it into two factors — a line connects it to the two numbers it splits into, like 60 down to 2 and 30. Keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime — those ending numbers are the prime factors.

3. Group the primes, make factors

Help

Any bunch of the prime factors, multiplied together, makes a factor of the whole number. Tap primes to put them in a group, make the group, and a box shows the factor you made and what it pairs with — like 100 = 25 × 4. Every group you make gets its own box.

How to factor a number

  • Primes are the building blocks: a prime number (2, 3, 5, 7, 11…) can only be divided evenly by 1 and itself. Every whole number above 1 that isn’t prime itself is primes multiplied together — 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.
  • Divide it down to prime factors: pick a prime that divides your number evenly and divide. Repeat with the answer until what’s left is prime itself — that’s the last factor. The answer is written two ways: repeated factors (2 × 2 × 3 × 5) and exponent form (22 × 3 × 5). The app multiplies the factors back to check they equal your number.
  • Factor tree: split the number into any two factors, then split those, until every ending number is prime. Different splits still land on the same primes — try splitting 60 as 6 × 10 or as 5 × 12 and compare.
  • Grouping: multiply any group of the prime factors and you get a factor of the number. Grouping [2 × 3] and [2 × 5] from 60’s primes gives 6 × 10 = 60, so 6 and 10 are factors. Every factor of a number can be built this way.
  • Blocks: if the blocks fill equal rows with none left over, the row size and the number of rows are a factor pair. If no row size works — only one row of all of them, or all rows of one — the number is prime. That’s the blocks’ way of teaching what prime means. Collect each prime you prove in your prime collection; find all 25 primes up to 100 and the blocks board extends to numbers up to 200.
  • Numbers run from 2 to 1,000,000. The number 1 has no prime factors — it isn’t prime and isn’t built from primes.

How to reduce a fraction

  • The whole reduction is one line: your fraction = its prime factors = the reduced fraction. The top and bottom numbers are written as products of primes, lined up one above the other: 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 over 90 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5.
  • A prime that appears on both top and bottom can be cancelled — tap one on top, then its match on the bottom. Cancelling is just dividing the top and bottom by the same number, which never changes the fraction’s value.
  • The reduced fraction at the end of the line changes each time a pair cancels. When no prime factor appears on both sides any more, the fraction is in lowest terms: 84/90 becomes 14/15.
  • The cancelled factors multiplied together give the greatest common factor of the two numbers — here 2 × 3 = 6, and indeed 84 ÷ 6 = 14 and 90 ÷ 6 = 15.
  • If everything cancels on one side, that side becomes 1. If both sides cancel completely, the fraction equals 1.
