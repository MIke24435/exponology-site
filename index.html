The first app: born at the mah jongg table

Learning mah jongg, I found the hardest part of the game wasn't the rules. It was looking at the tiles in my hand and trying to pick which hand to go for.

App No. 1

The Mah Jongg Hand Finder

Tell it what's in your rack. It tells you which hands are still within reach.

The card lists dozens of hands. Matching them against thirteen tiles in your head — while the Charleston moves and the table waits — is not easy. In fact, it's frustrating. So the Finder does the matching for you.

  • All 79 hands from the complete 2026 National Mah Jongg League card, each listed separately, just as the card lists them.
  • Search by what you're holding — suits, numbers, points, and the flowers, dragons, winds, pairs, pongs, kongs, and quints your hand needs.
  • Made for the middle of a game — when your first plan dies, re-search in seconds and pick a new hand before your next tile.
  • Runs on any phone or computer — nothing to install, nothing to sign up for.

App No. 2

The Mah Jongg Hand Matcher

Put in the tiles you're actually holding. It ranks the hands you could still make.

The Hand Finder searches the card by what you're looking for. The Matcher starts from the other end: enter your actual rack — 13 tiles, or 14 if you're East — and it lines your tiles up against all 79 hands on the 2026 National Mah Jongg League card, ranked by how close you are.

  • Three counts for every hand — how many of its tiles you Have, how many Jokers you're using, and how many you Don't Have yet, so the closest hands sort themselves to the top.
  • A gold star ★ next to the hand's name when it needs singles or pairs — the tiles a Joker can't stand in for, so you know before you commit.
  • A Random hand button deals you a practice rack to try it with — 14 tiles when East is checked, 13 otherwise.
  • Help built in — how to use the Matcher, and how to read the hands it shows you.

App No. 3

CipherDesk

Newspaper cryptograms, desk edition — fill in the blanks, one letter at a time.

The kind of puzzle that used to sit beside the crossword: a short quote, encoded. Each letter stands for another letter, never itself — work out which is which, and the quote reveals itself.

  • The classic rule, kept simple — fill in the blanks over each cipher letter, with a substitution map that tracks every guess as you go.
  • Help when you want it — Hint, Check, Reset, and Undo, plus a timer, dark mode, and print, so a puzzle works on screen or on paper.
  • Pick by mood — categories Wisdom, Science, Leadership, Wit, History, Sports, and Comedy, plus All and Saved.
  • Bring your own, and keep favorites — paste a cryptogram, or type a solution and scramble it into a puzzle, then keep the ones you like in a Saved library.

Built by a person. Built with an AI.

None of these apps would exist without two ingredients: a frustration I ran into myself, and an AI agent to help me build the fix. Here's how each one happens.

1

Spot the frustration

It starts in real life — at the mah jongg table, staring at a rack of tiles with no clear hand to chase. If a moment like that keeps repeating, it's a candidate.

2

Build it with an AI agent

I describe what I need and what "working" looks like. The AI helps design it, write it, and revise it — I steer, test, and decide what ships. It's a partnership, not a vending machine.

3

Use it myself

Every app here has to earn its place in my own life first — used at the table, in the middle of real games. If it doesn't actually help, it doesn't belong on this site.
